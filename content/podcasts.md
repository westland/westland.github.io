### About the Podcast

*Westland on Technology* is an insightful audio and essay series exploring the fascinating history of technology, corporate strategy, innovation economics, and the often-accidental breakthroughs that shaped the modern world. 

Hosted by technologist and decision-sciences professor **J. Christopher Westland**, each episode delves into the analytical principles, human drama, and economic forces behind seminal technological revolutions. 

Key themes and episodes explore:
- **The Accidental Revolution:** How the demise of audio cassette tapes in the early 1990s left Sony with excess manufacturing plants that were brilliantly repurposed to coat electrodes—giving birth to the modern lithium-ion polymer battery.
- **The Art & Math of Bebop Jazz:** How Charlie Parker and post-war bebop innovators exploited chord contrafacts on popular standards (like Gershwin’s *I Got Rhythm*) to create revolutionary jazz while dodging royalties.
- **Niche Innovation Survival:** Why vacuum tube amplification survived the transistor revolution and thrives today as a luxury high-end audio market.
- **Cryptography & Computational Inflexions:** Alan Turing, Marian Rejewski, and the breaking of Enigma at Bletchley Park.
- **Network Economics & Bubble Cycles:** From the 1840s British Railway Mania and the fall of MySpace to modern social platforms.
- **Everyday Inventions:** The curious, unfair history of screws, fasteners, typewriter keyboards (QWERTY), and office cubicles.

### Episode Description

In this featured guest interview on the *Million Dollar Stories Podcast*, Professor Chris Westland joins host Mike Fallat to discuss his acclaimed Springer book *Audit Analytics: Data Science for the Accounting Profession*. 

Professor Westland examines the growing crisis in accounting valuation—highlighting how the audited "Book Value" of corporations has dramatically decoupled from actual market capitalizations for leading tech companies (often accounting for less than 10% of total valuation). He discusses how modern open-source analytical ecosystems—particularly **R** and **Python**—are revitalizing financial analysis and forensic audit capabilities, empowering analysts to uncover fraud, model anomalies, and evaluate complex datasets.
